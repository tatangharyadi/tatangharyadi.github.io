Digital Transformation Chief Technology Officer, Sophie Paris Indonesia

A B2B2C e-commerce transformation three years behind schedule, and a USD 3 million ERP and WMS migration that wasn't going to land, so the first job was triage.

The rescue turned on scope. The programme was replacing ERP and WMS together, an ambitious combination for a project already three years late. I implemented the new WMS and kept the legacy ERP for financials and manufacturing. The warehouse was where the pain and the return actually were. The financial modules already worked. That split is what realised the ROI on a project already written off, and it's the decision I would point to first — finishing meant stopping part of the work.

The hard part of that decision had nothing to do with the technology. A programme three years in has a sunk cost that everyone can name and a set of commitments people have already defended in front of a board. Proposing to deliver less than was promised reads as conceding failure at exactly the moment nobody wants to. I made it possible to carry by separating the two systems on evidence: the warehouse was where the operational pain and the measurable return both sat, and the financial modules weren't the thing anyone was complaining about. Pulling back on the part that was already working was the easier case to make, so that's the direction I argued.

Re-engineering the IT function was the other half. A programme that's missed for three years is usually organised to produce status reports, regardless of who's staffed on it. Changing who decided what, and shortening the distance between a problem being seen and being fixed, is what gave a nine-month plan its credibility. The same team largely delivered it.

The transformation then completed in nine months after three years of not completing, on an IT function re-engineered to deliver.

From there the work compounded on what was already fixed. The distributor point-of-sale system needed refactoring, and the e-commerce, point-of-sale and loyalty channels needed one unified omnichannel UX, which is what moved customer lifetime value. Giving a distributor a 360 view took consolidating disparate sales, stock and assortment data into PowerBI dashboards. That's the reporting nobody could assemble while the data sat in separate systems.

Finally, harmonising IT systems across affiliates in Vietnam, Malaysia and the Philippines with a global cross-functional team, so regional expansion had something to expand onto. Social commerce across Indonesia, Vietnam and the Philippines scaled to 1.5 million monthly active users.
